The format

What you get each week

Every issue follows the same order, so you can see at a glance what changed and read further only where it matters to your practice.

  1. First in every issue

    Rule changes this week

    A short list of the opinions that change or clarify a rule of law, each with a one-line note on what moved and a link to the opinion. When nothing changed, the digest says so. Supreme Court orders, including rule amendments, are not covered; each issue links to the Court’s orders page.

  2. Then, for each opinion

    Every new published opinion

    Each newly published opinion from the Supreme Court and the Court of Appeals, headed by its opinion number, filing date, author, and disposition, and followed by:

    Takeaway
    The practical point, in one sentence.
    Holding
    The question decided, the rule applied, and the result, in two to four sentences.
    What changed
    Whether the opinion changes, clarifies, or reaffirms the law, and how. Or, plainly: “No change to existing law.”
    Why it matters
    A practice tip: the concrete reason a South Carolina practitioner should care.
    Practice areas
    Short labels, such as Insurance, Criminal, or Family, so you can scan for your field.
    Full opinion
    A direct link to the opinion PDF, because the summary is never the last word.

Method

How it works

Each issue is assembled automatically from public sources, in three steps.

  1. 1

    Collected

    Newly published opinions from the South Carolina Judicial Branch are retrieved through CourtListener, a free legal-data service run by the nonprofit Free Law Project.

  2. 2

    Summarized

    Claude, the AI model made by Anthropic, writes each summary from that opinion’s text alone. It is instructed not to draw on outside knowledge, to cite only authorities that appear in the opinion, and to write “unclear from the opinion” rather than guess.

  3. 3

    Delivered

    The week’s summaries are assembled into one email and sent through Buttondown, usually on Wednesday or Thursday, with a link to every full opinion. If an opinion posts late or a summary wasn’t ready, a short supplement follows on Thursday.

When an opinion is too long to summarize in full, its entry says how much of the text the summary is based on. If a summary can’t be produced, the opinion is still listed with a link to read it.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is it free?

Yes. The digest costs nothing, and your email address is used only to send it.

How accurate are the summaries?

They are a careful starting point, not an authority. Claude summarizes each opinion from its own text and is instructed to say “unclear from the opinion” rather than guess, but AI-generated summaries can still contain errors or omissions. Treat every summary as a guide to the opinion, never a substitute for reading it.

Which courts are covered?

Published opinions of the Supreme Court of South Carolina and the South Carolina Court of Appeals. Unpublished opinions and Supreme Court orders (including rule amendments) are not included; each issue links to the Supreme Court’s orders page.

When does it arrive?

Usually once a week, on Wednesday or Thursday. The digest checks for new opinions on Wednesday afternoon and again on Thursday morning (Eastern Time); if an opinion posts late, or a summary was not available in Wednesday’s issue, a short supplement follows. If no new published opinions were released, there is no email that week.

How do I unsubscribe?

Every email ends with an unsubscribe link from Buttondown, the service that sends the newsletter. Use it at any time and you will stop receiving the digest.

Is this legal advice?

No. The digest is for informational purposes only. It is not legal advice, it does not create an attorney-client relationship, and it is no substitute for reading the opinion and applying your own professional judgment.
